Sebi's zero-tax pitch to bring foreign investors back

By Abhijeet ToshniwalFirst published on LinkedIn, 3 November 2026

Foreign investors have pulled a record ~$31 billion out of Indian stocks in 2026. Now the market regulator is pitching a zero-tax fix.

Picture a fund manager in Singapore. She can buy Taiwan's chip stocks, Korea's AI plays, or India.

On Indian shares held over a year, she pays 12.5% long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax. If she sells within a year, it's 20%.

On Monday, Reuters reported that Sebi has recommended, in its pre-Budget wishlist, that the government scrap LTCG tax for foreign portfolio investors (FPIs). It also wants switches between mutual fund schemes (units held 1+ year) not to count as a taxable sale.

How I connect the dots

  1. No LTCG
  2. → foreign investors' returns rise by ~2.3 percentage points (IIFL estimate)
  3. → India looks better against other emerging markets on an after-tax basis
  4. → more dollars could come back, supporting the rupee and the large caps FPIs own most
  5. → but the government gives up revenue: equity LTCG brought in ₹1.3 trillion in FY25

Markets noticed. LTCG hopes were one of the reasons cited as the Sensex jumped 1,233 points on Monday.

A tax cut opens the door, but earnings growth is what brings money in. And there's a fairness test coming. If a foreign fund pays 0% while an Indian SIP investor pays 12.5%, expect a loud debate before the Budget in February.

For now it's a proposal, and it isn't binding. The government decides.

Should India scrap LTCG for foreigners only, for everyone, or not at all?

Sources: Reuters via Business Standard, 2 Nov 2026; Business Today, 2 Nov 2026; Tribune/ANI, 2 Nov 2026.
