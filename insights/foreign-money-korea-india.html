Why foreign money sold Korea and bought India on the same day

By Abhijeet ToshniwalFirst published on LinkedIn, 4 November 2026

On Tuesday, foreign investors sold 1.52 trillion of Korean stocks. The same day, they bought ₹6,837 crore of Indian ones. Same money. Opposite directions. Here's why.

On 3 November the KOSPI fell 3.6%. Samsung and SK hynix, the memory-chip giants riding the AI boom, dropped 4 to 5%.

The trigger? Growing unease about how US Big Tech is paying for its AI build-out. Oracle alone is raising $20 billion through stock and bonds.

Mumbai, same day: Nifty up 1.6%, Sensex up 1,225 points, a fourth straight gain. FIIs posted their biggest single-day buying since October 2025. FMCG led, not tech.

How I connect the dots

  1. AI spending doubts
  2. → money exits chip-heavy markets like Korea
  3. → funds look for growth that doesn't depend on AI hardware
  4. → India's mix (banks, consumption, autos) suddenly looks like a hedge
  5. → FII inflows support large caps and the rupee (~94.7/$)

For two years India was under-owned partly because it had no AI chip story. That same gap is now the attraction. Easing crude and decent Q2 earnings helped too.

But two good days are not a trend. Analysts are calling these early signs, not a reversal. If oil spikes again or the AI trade bounces back, this money can turn just as fast.

Takeaway: in global markets, what you don't own matters as much as what you do.
