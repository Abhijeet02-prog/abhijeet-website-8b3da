October's GST: India wants to spend. Can it keep spending?

By Abhijeet ToshniwalFirst published on LinkedIn, 2 November 2026

₹2.31 lakh crore. That's how much GST India collected in October 2026. It's up 22.9% from a year ago.

Picture a car showroom this Navratri and Dussehra season: families booking SUVs so they can drive home before Diwali. The numbers match that picture:

  • Maruti Suzuki: highest-ever monthly domestic sales, up 20%
  • Hyundai: a record month, up 16.7%
  • Tata Motors (passenger vehicles): up 21%

How I connect the dots

  • Event: festive buying pushes up big-ticket purchases
  • Economy: domestic GST up 15.8%, GST on imports up 42.2%, net GST up 25.6%
  • Market: Nifty rose 2% last week as foreign investors (FIIs) turned net buyers

My read: don't treat the 22.9% as pure demand. Domestic GST (up 15.8%) is the cleaner signal of what Indians are actually buying. Part of the 42% jump on imports may just be a weaker rupee making every import cost more in rupee terms.

Stronger tax collections also give the government more room on its deficit, and bond markets watch that.

The real test comes after Diwali. Does demand hold in November and December, or did the festive calendar pull October's buying forward?

Takeaway: festive data shows India wants to spend. Post-festive data shows whether it can keep spending.

Sources: IANS, 1 Nov 2026; Bombay Samachar, 1 Nov 2026; Business Today, 1 Nov 2026; Autocar India, 1 Nov 2026; Liquide weekly market review, 1 Nov 2026.
