Oil is flowing again. So why did it just touch $118?

By Abhijeet ToshniwalFirst published on LinkedIn, 31 October 2026

Picture a tanker crossing the Strait of Hormuz this week. Gulf crude exports are back above pre-war levels.

But on Friday, a tanker in the strait was hit by explosives attached to a small boat. So the barrels still arrive. They just cost far more to move. Al Jazeera reports freight near $40 a barrel, about ten times normal, driven by war-risk insurance.

How I connect the dots

  1. Hormuz attacks
  2. → insurance and freight jump; Brent touched ~$118 on 30 Oct
  3. → India relies heavily on imported crude, so it needs more dollars: pressure on the rupee and on inflation
  4. → the RBI had already raised the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.50% on 7 Oct, its first hike in nearly four years
  5. → markets: Nifty rose 0.51% on Friday, lifted by IT (+2.3%) and pharma, sectors that earn in dollars; energy and PSU banks fell over 1%, and midcaps and smallcaps slid

My read: a green index can hide a nervous market. When exporters lead, the market is quietly pricing in costlier oil and a costlier dollar.

Next on the watchlist: the US midterms on 3 November. Traders fear the war could intensify after them.

Is your portfolio built for $118 oil, or for the oil prices we had before this war?

Sources: Al Jazeera, 30 Oct 2026; The Nation/Reuters data, 31 Oct 2026; HDFC Sky market close, 30 Oct 2026; Business Today, 7 Oct 2026.
